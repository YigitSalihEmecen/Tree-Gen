TreeGen

Procedural 3D tree generation in the browser

TreeGen builds real 3D tree models from botanical parameters. Every vertex, every bark texture and every leaf is computed in the browser — there is not a single image or model file behind any of it.

Created by

Yigit Salih Emecen

Design, engineering and the parameter sets for the hand-tuned species.

What you can do with it

  • Sixteen species presets, from a quaking aspen to a saguaro cactus, each a full parameter set you can then take apart.
  • Around sixty live controls — trunk taper and flare, per-level branching, crown envelope, foliage, bark and epiphytes — all regenerating as you drag.
  • Four seasons, thirty-one leaf shapes with five venation patterns, twelve bark styles, two themes.
  • Experimental forms, off by default: turn it up and trunks start running horizontally for a stretch before elbowing back toward the light, throwing sharp kinks, diving, or corkscrewing — the fallen-and-resprouted shapes.
  • A moss, lichen and bracket-fungus system that grows real 3D forms onto the bark — five moss forms, four fungus forms, placed by where they would actually take hold.
  • Randomise rolls an entirely new tree: growth habit, structure, foliage, bark and epiphytes.
  • Export to GLB (geometry plus the generated textures), OBJ, STL for printing, a PNG of the viewport, or the parameter set as JSON.

Sharing

The address bar always holds the tree you are looking at — species, seed, season and theme. Copy the URL and it opens exactly the same tree.

Built with three.js. No build step, no bundler, no assets.

Two independent generators feed one mesh stage. Both come from published computer-graphics research; the implementations here follow the papers' own equations.

1 · Parametric model

A tree is a recursion of stems. The trunk is a chain of segments, each rotated away from the last by a curvature angle; stems split into clones along their length, and spawn child branches whose length follows a crown-shape function. Radii come from a length ratio raised to a falloff power, tapered by one of four continuously blended modes. A flare equation swells the root buttress, a sinusoidal term gives non-circular cross sections, and an optional envelope prunes the crown to a chosen silhouette.

Jason Weber and Joseph Penn, “Creation and Rendering of Realistic Trees”, SIGGRAPH ’95, pp. 119–128. ACM DL · PDF

Four of the presets — Quaking Aspen, Black Tupelo, Weeping Willow and California Black Oak — are the parameter sets published in that paper's appendix.

2 · Space colonization

The crown volume is seeded with attraction points. Every growth node within an influence radius of a point is pulled toward the normalised sum of its attractors and grows one step in that direction; points that come within a kill distance are consumed. The competition between buds for space is what produces asymmetric, naturally uneven canopies that pure recursion cannot. Branch thickness follows the pipe model.

Adam Runions, Brendan Lane and Przemyslaw Prusinkiewicz, “Modeling Trees with a Space Colonization Algorithm”, Eurographics Workshop on Natural Phenomena, 2007. PDF

3 · Turning a skeleton into a mesh

Branch centrelines are smoothed with a Catmull–Rom spline and swept as generalized cylinders using parallel-transport frames, so the cross section never twists along the branch. Every stem is closed at both ends and its base is buried inside its parent with a swelling collar, the way a real branch collar welds a limb to a trunk. Leaves are genuine polygons — one parametric width function covers ovate through pinnate outlines and a polar lobe function covers palmate ones — cupped and drooped in three dimensions, then merged into a single buffer so a thirty-thousand-leaf canopy is one draw call.

4 · Moss, lichen and fungi

Epiphytes are placed by score, not scattered. Every candidate site on the bark is rated — moss for the shaded side of the trunk, the lower stretch of it, the upper surfaces of near-horizontal limbs and thick wood over twigs; bracket fungi for vertical wood, sideways-facing surfaces and the lower trunk — with a 3D value-noise term so the growth clumps into patches instead of freckling the whole tree. The best-scoring sites are then used first, so turning the coverage down thins the growth back toward the spots where it would really be. Flat forms are bent around the branch before they are placed, because a rigid disc on a curved limb reads as a blade sticking out of the bark. Spreading mats, carpets, velvet fuzz, low cushions, foliose lichen, hanging strands, shelf conks, turkey-tail fans, cap clusters and cup fungi are all built as 3D meshes and merged into one buffer each.

5 · Textures

Bark is tileable anisotropic value-noise fBm, drawn to a canvas and turned into colour, a Sobel-derived normal map and a roughness map. Leaf venation is drawn. The sky is a gradient run through a prefiltered environment map so the physically-based materials have something to reflect.
